Zero Middleman Architecture

Who Owns Your Data? You Do.

Subscription SaaS platforms pool thousands of landlords into a single shared database and hold your financial ledgers hostage behind recurring monthly fees. Rentlyo does the opposite.

Pillar 1: Data Sovereignty

Direct Google Firebase Project Ownership

When we deploy Rentlyo for your property, the database is provisioned on your own Google account. Rentlyo (the developer) does not hold, inspect, or intermediate your tenant records, rent amounts, phone numbers, or ledger balances.

  • ✓ No shared multi-tenant vulnerability — your records are physically isolated in your own cloud container.
  • ✓ Zero risk of vendor lock-in — you hold the Firebase admin keys and can revoke developer access at any moment.
  • ✓ Permanent operation — even if Rentlyo were to vanish tomorrow, your Android apps communicate directly with Google Firebase and will continue functioning forever.

Traditional SaaS vs. Rentlyo Architecture

Typical Subscription SaaS:

Tenant App ➔ Vendor's Central Server ➔ Shared DB ➔ Admin App.
If you stop paying ₹2,000/month, vendor cuts off your access and locks your history.

Rentlyo Direct Architecture:

Tenant App ➔ Your Google Firebase Instance ➔ Admin App.
Zero middleman server. Zero subscription check. You own the infrastructure.

Security is verified cryptographically by Google Cloud before any document is returned over HTTPS.

Pillar 2: Tenant Isolation

Mathematical Unit Isolation & Access Control

Can Tenant A see Tenant B's rent, agreement, or phone number? Never. Every request from the Tenant App is signed with Firebase Authentication tokens and evaluated against declarative Firestore rules.

  • ✓ Tenants have read-only access exclusively to their specific deal document and utility bills.
  • ✓ Administrative actions (creating units, adjusting tariffs, confirming offline payments) require cryptographic owner claim tokens.
  • ✓ Commercial / Residential partitioning ensures shopping plaza shops and private flats remain isolated inside separate property contexts.
