Rent Agreement Checklist for Indian Landlords: 8 Clauses You Can't Afford to Skip

By Aaryan Gupta (Software Engineer) • 8 Min Read • Indian Real Estate Law Best Practices

Whether you own a commercial market plaza, residential flats, or a student PG hostel, a casual oral lease or downloaded generic agreement template exposes you to expensive disputes. Here is the operational checklist every Indian landlord needs before handing over the keys.

The 8 Non-Negotiable Clauses

1. Explicit Rent Calculation & Grace Period

Specify the precise due date (e.g., 1st or 5th of every English calendar month), acceptable payment channels (UPI intent directly to landlord bank account), and grace period before late penalties trigger.

2. Utility Sub-Meter Formula Clause

For buildings with shared main electricity meters and private sub-meters, state the exact tariff rate per unit (kWh) and whether generator backup or water pump usage is charged per flat or per unit.

3. Security Deposit Forfeiture & Adjustment Rules

Explicitly forbid tenants from adjusting the last month's rent against their security deposit without written landlord consent. Detail deduction criteria for paint restoration or unpaid electric bills.

4. Step-Up Rent Escalation Tiers

For 11-month or multi-year commercial leases, specify future rent increases in advance (e.g., 5% or 7% increase upon renewal). Rentlyo has built-in step-up tiers to automate this.

5. Permissible Use & Commercial / Residential Isolation

Define whether the premises may be used for retail, office storage, or solely private residential occupancy. Forbid unapproved subletting.

6. Maintenance Responsibility Thresholds

Clarify minor vs major repairs: minor repairs under ₹500 (fused bulbs, tap washers) handled by tenant; major structural repairs handled by landlord.

7. Lock-In Period and Notice Period

Specify the mandatory lock-in period (e.g., 6 months) and required written notice period (e.g., 30 days) prior to vacating.

8. Shared Digital Ledger Acknowledgment

Both parties agree that payment reporting and balance acknowledgments recorded in the property's management app constitute the operational record of tenancy.
